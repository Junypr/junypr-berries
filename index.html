dead fish eyes

A man sits in a room that is white and square. Soft light emanates from all four walls, and it fades once a day so that he may sleep, but there is no window, so he does not really know what time it is. The nurse that brings him meals thrice a day says it's been four months. He feels like it's been longer.

She has set his first meal of the day — breakfast, if one can call it that — on a low table. There is a fillet of overcooked tilapia, a bowl of spinach and kale, a glass of water, and a handful of pink pills. There is no coffee, because that might harm the child. No salt or salad dressing, because that, too, would be unhealthy. But at least they’ve tried to give him some variety.

He sits on the ground, cross-legged, and eats with the single fork they’ve given. There is no knife. There has never been a knife. Nor other blades, nor ropes, nor hooks, nor bars, in the room. He has checked.

HELLO BEAUTIFUL

It’s a Monday, and you’re grumpy. You’re in your cubicle with your third cup of watery coffee, writing yet another script to analyze last week’s data. A year ago, you dreamed of leading the experiment, but now, fresh out of college and freshly rejected from every PhD program you’d hoped to enter, you find yourself stuck in a part time job at a radio telescope array. You think you might apply again next year. The prospect doesn’t seem so hopeful.

“Hello, beautiful,” a voice says in your ear. You bolt upright. The only person who’s ever said that to you was also the only girl you’ve ever woken up next to, but it can’t be Cass. You haven’t talked to her in two months.

“Hello, beautiful.” The voice is almost like the monotone of Sunny, the voice assistant in Ocula — the earpiece and contact lenses augmented reality set everyone wears. Except this voice’s upbeat tone sounds genuine. And Sunny never called you beautiful.

“Yes, you. With the blue button-down. Obviously.”

Like Father

Abby balanced the newest letter on top of the neat stack of business envelopes banished to the bottom drawer of her desk. Each had the same return address and name: Mark Li, her father. She’d been promising herself that she’d read them someday. Just not today. Every time she saw his type-writer handwriting, she remembered their final conversation. “I never loved you,” she’d said. It’d been three years, but the heat of those words still rose to her cheeks in a flush.

She cradled a steaming cup of tea and frowned at the tower. The handwriting on the newest letter was shaky, like the scrawl of a child just learning to write, and the corners of the envelope were crumpled. Mark hadn’t been the most attentive to detail, but he knew the value of a first impression. Abby pinched the bridge of her nose, flicked open a pocket knife, and slid the blade under the envelope flap. Maybe today was the day.

Jupiter

When I think of you,
I try to picture you in New York
though the strongest memories
the sweetest,
are of kids in khakis
you and your bear paw hands
eating crackers and cheese
the way you watched the pink-gold of the sunrise
reflect off the stucco
off the puddles
off the brown-black of your artist’s eyes
beautiful.

But to imagine you then is to erase
who you are now
scarred from the scratches I tried to heal
though they never cut too deep
I try not to think —
of the days when you were falling too fast to catch
spiraling down the sky

I blame myself for not knowing
even if I was too young — I wasn’t
and it scares me
knowing me, knowing you,
how it could’ve been me
shaking, screaming into the heedless night
more alike than the friends that fled
ignorant of the weight of expectation
jealous because you shined so bright
you, Jupiter on earth,
New York City of the night sky
didn’t anyone ever tell you?
you are beautiful

Suburbia, USA

Hello again, and again
the humid heat sticks to my skin
like the sap of square-cut rosemary bushes
too scared to exhale their scent
smile and nod.

How could four months do this to me?
a shadow among the fuzzy pinks and teals
empty, black
I walk too fast, talk too little
don't want a drink, don't want to go bowling
Good afternoon, how are you doing?

the only thing that lives here are the ducks
frantically paddling beneath the surface
I think anything bigger gets shot
What am I looking at?
Oh, just the hawk with the pigeon in its claws
The one you just startled
Guess you didn't notice

The birds sing regulation songs
for storybook families
in cages of regulation stucco walls
they scream in their sleep
we were like that once
Happy new year to you too.
is anything new?

I don't

It's easy to admit.
The words escape before I register their heartlessness.
They taste of copper pennies and thin blood
like your promises of eternity.
I've freed myself from the shackles of "and,"
"mine" and "yours" no longer;
I exist in singularity,
the period on the last page,
unpaired, but not lonely.
It's what I needed —
the starless sky of twilight
to fly as I will
and space to fall.
I don't
miss you.

Child's Play

leaves drift past the concrete facade
they fly
then disappear beneath rubber tires
dust and exhaust
I rake the survivors off the ground with my fingertips
bone shards of their skeletons in my hair
I could build a pile big enough to bury a body
if childhood starts when I jump into the first pile of leaves
then can it be said that I was ever a child until now?
if adulthood starts when we first confront death
then can it be said that I was ever an adult until now?
spare me mortality, but keep not from me the changing of the seasons
nature's little death and resurrection
is just child's play

superposition

Despite the months I still can trace your paths
within my mind like feet once walked each week,
and gravel crunching underfoot still brings
the scent of rain-soaked dirt, and tulips raised
in earthen pots like drops of sunlit blood,
and chains of purple blooms to tempt the bees
with springs soft baby's breath. With eyes half closed
the emerald moss that glimmers in stone cracks
bejewels my maple's slender arms, and sun
reflected off the river conjures hoards
of honking geese alighting on the glass
lagoons for meager scraps of bread. Thus fog
unrolled among the concrete crowns
and pillars might unfurl slow over peaks
all blanketed with black green pines, and the
first chills of fall are home's dead winter's cold.
The east is cloaked in a Pacific haze,
and I relive with every breath and step
and sight the sounds and smells of long-loved home.
